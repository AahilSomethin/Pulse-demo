# Limitations

Each item is classified as resolved and verified, partially resolved, blocked by missing data, blocked by redistribution permissions, or an inherent methodological limitation. The system overview is [FLOW.md](FLOW.md).

## Resolved and verified

- **Quoted FollowMe rows.** `device_date` and `timestamp` are quoted and can contain a comma in other exports. The parser splits quotes and stitches fields. A leading BOM is stripped.
- **Which clock is the observation.** `device_date` orders the map. `timestamp` is the database save time. It breaks ties and feeds the lag statistic. Median lag on the August extract is about 2 seconds.
- **Duplicate positions.** Exact repeats of device, time, latitude, and longitude are dropped and counted. Day 1 removed 6,090. The month removed 197,769.
- **Heading.** The field varies across the extract. It is shown as a tracker attribute. It is not a route.
- **Inferred areas versus places.** Dwell clusters use a 0.05° cell and the label “Inferred area”. They are not harbours, islands, or terminals. A named link is created only when both ends fall in a polygon that has a name.
- **Historical labelling.** Explore, Network, and Simulate state that positions are tracker reports, not live vessels and not a dispatch.
- **Same-island cell pairs.** A passage between different 0.05° cells on one named island is flagged and counted. It leaves the inter-island list, the map, and opportunity ranking. The same polygon id matches. So does the same island name when the atolls do not conflict. The same name in two atolls stays a crossing. The Thilafushi pair described in [CONNECTIVITY_METHOD.md](CONNECTIVITY_METHOD.md) is this case. The passages are not deleted.
- **Explicit time zones.** A `device_date` that already ends in `Z` or a numeric offset is parsed as that instant. A value with no zone is still read as UTC, which is how the August extract was interpreted.
- **Map clutter.** Connections draws a zoom-limited subset of thin lines and caps how many share one endpoint. Find opportunities draws the selected opportunity, or a short faded set when none is selected. Route Lab draws the selected connection. The lists still contain every matching record. Drawing fewer lines does not drop a connection from the data.

## Partially resolved

- **Real FollowMe in the app.** One daily file loads, plays, and matches the offline count for day 1 (435,033 kept rows, 1,432 devices). Previous and next day work in development against `data/FollowMe/FollowMe_august_2025`. The full month is not held in the browser. A production build does not serve those files; the user selects them.
- **Unusual journey times.** A credible passage is flagged only when the cohort has at least 8 journeys and 3 vessels, the median absolute deviation is above zero, the robust score exceeds 3.5, and the duration exceeds the third quartile plus three interquartile ranges. One long journey among identical times is not flagged. Barely-moving, direct-but-slow, and long-path labels use distance over time. They are leads for replay. They are not delays, incidents, or collisions. Find opportunities promotes at most three of those leads, from different connections. A place name comes before an unnamed pair. A repeated connection comes before a one-off. A journey that is also long for that device comes before a higher robust score. Barely-moving passages, reporting holes, tracks with fewer than four positions, paths of six times the straight-line separation or more, and times that already match that device are not promoted. The minimum-journeys box does not change this check. The August file can count long durations but cannot replay them or name the device. There is no vessel type, so a slower vessel can look unusual. Regenerated on 24 October 2026, that file has 765 connections and 122 same-island passages kept beside it. The app flags 293 of its credible durations. 53 connections have at least two, which is what the full findings list requires before it repeats a connection. Day 1, compared with that summary, has three replayable journeys. Review first keeps two: Jetty #7 to Kuda Huraa, about 89 minutes against a usual 31 minutes, from 181 journeys and 33 vessels, and a longer track toward Maafushi. The third is the same Jetty #7 connection, so it is not repeated as a separate card. Day 10’s barely-moving Hulhulé to Malé journeys stay in the flag count and are not promoted. The Himmafushi longer track is the one Review first keeps. Days 17 and 25 have flagged times and nothing left to promote.
- **Tracking-quality jumps.** Report-to-report segments over 40 knots and 200 m are classed with the reported speed and with an out-and-back check. A high reported speed is fast recorded movement. A low reported speed fits a bad position better. On day 1, using the same distance function as the app, 669 jumps cleared both gates: 588 also had a reported speed of at least 30, 42 stayed under 15, 39 were in between, and 23 were followed by a point that returned close to the earlier position. The day’s existing 40-knot count is 3,212 and still includes shorter jumps. None of this is an accident or a collision. 520 of 1,416 devices with a gap have a typical gap of 15 minutes or more, so short movements on those devices are easy to miss.
- **Journey reconstruction.** Dwells and the passages between them are computed per loaded day. A passage whose straight-line speed exceeds 40 knots, or whose clock does not advance, is a coverage gap. A passage whose longest report gap exceeds 30 minutes is sparse. Both stay in the record and out of the typical time. Passages that cross midnight are in the August summary, which carries each vessel's open stop into the next file. The day view does not. The 40-knot gate uses nautical miles from the coordinates. It does not convert the speed column.
- **Vessel denominators.** Evidence still uses distinct devices on any passage between the cells, including flagged ones, because that is the count stored in the August file. A loaded day also shows how many of those devices have a credible passage. The month file has no device ids, so it cannot de-duplicate vessels across connections or split flagged-only devices.
- **Route Lab.** The comparison is the pooled typical time, the middle half of times, the longer journeys when the sample allows, straight-line separation, and the tracked-path ratio. A typed duration is hypothetical. It is not a schedule and it is not a navigable path. The map shows that connection.
- **Census 2022.** `data/Census 2022/P5.xlsx` is Maldives Bureau of Statistics Table P5, resident population by island and sex, 2022. `npm run data:census` checks that the 186 admin-island rows sum to the sheet total of 236,911 and writes `public/reference/census-2022.json`, including 9 Malé ward rows that are not part of that total. Against the current OpenStreetMap islands, 126 rows match (111 by a name that is unique on both sides, 15 by atoll and name), 12 duplicate names stay ambiguous, and 69 rows stay unmatched (61 of them admin islands). The profile says which method matched. A unique-name match is not presented as an atoll-and-name confirmation. A conflicting atoll is not overridden. There is no isolation score and no journeys-per-resident figure. Census `code` is empty on the shipped table, so the code path does not fire for this file. Dhivehi names are searchable and are not used to force a census match.
- **Light, dark, and small screens.** Both themes use the same semantic roles. Light and dark were both rendered. At a 390px-wide viewport and at 640×500 CSS pixels with a 2× device pixel ratio, the page had no horizontal overflow. The header wraps, the sidebar scrolls, and the shell does not use a fixed dashboard height. A separate browser zoom control at 125%, 150%, and 200% was not driven independently of those viewport sizes.
- **Reference geography on the map.** Island polygons can be selected, highlighted, and labelled from about zoom 7. Maldives returns to the nationwide camera. Atoll zoom uses the polygons that carry that code. The file has 668 named island or islet polygons, English names on 657, Dhivehi names on 219, and an atoll code on 54. It is not a complete official island list.

## Blocked by missing data

- **Island and atoll completeness.** Seven admin boundaries (R, B, M, F, Th, L, Gn) had no geometry in the Overpass response, so islands there keep a null atoll and atoll zoom is unavailable for those codes. OpenStreetMap names are only as complete as that extract.
- **Hospitals and Coast Guard sites.** No verified medical or coast-guard layer is present. The evacuation leg stays off. A GeoJSON feature is used only when `kind` is `medical` and `verified` is true. SeaPulse does not check that claim against an authority.
- **Harbour verification.** The 56 harbour, pier, and ferry-terminal points are OpenStreetMap reference locations. None is an authority port-call layer. Search labels them that way.
- **MTCC workbook.** `data/MTCC/MTCC.xlsx` states that its passenger figures are randomly generated samples. It is not used. Frequency in Network is observed tracker movement, not a scheduled passenger service.
- **Speed unit.** The file does not name the unit. Values are shown as recorded. The month median of values at or above 0.5 is 9, from a sample of up to 80,000. The month maximum is 770. Nothing is converted to knots. If the recorded number is not on the scale the original viewer treated as a stop below 0.5, dwell detection and every later ranking can change. That assumption is labelled. It is not removed.
- **Custom date ranges.** Passage counts can use the per-day slots in a loaded window. Duration median, interquartile range, and 90th percentile stay on the full window. They are not derived from a month total or from daily counts alone.
- **Distinct vessels in the August file.** Per-connection vessel counts exist. A union across connections does not, because device ids are not stored.

## Blocked by redistribution permissions

- **Month summary.** `data/derived/connectivity-august-2025.json` is built offline and gitignored. Redistribution of cell centres derived from the private extract was not confirmed. Development serves it. Production returns 404. A production build cannot demonstrate the August connection graph. Count-only quality figures in `src/data/followme-quality.json` are the committed aggregate.
- **Connection coordinates in export.** CSV and print include labels, counts, durations, evidence, method, and limitations. They omit latitude, longitude, device identifiers, and trajectories.

## Inherent methodological limitations

- **Coverage is the file.** 1,776 devices across August 2025 are the tracked set. They are not the Maldivian fleet. A place with no ping is not proof that it has no service. Census population is context beside that record, not proof of demand or of insufficient service.
- **Straight-line distance.** Hubs, journeys, Route Lab, and Emergency use straight-line distance. Reefs, channels, traffic, and waiting are not in the data.
- **Boat operator consent.** The Boat operator page is a demo. The operator is one tracked device chosen in the browser. Consent means that device has a recorded straight-line passage within the emergency radius of a hypothetical pin. It is not a live position, not a commitment to sail, and not a rescue assignment.
- **Hubs are grid counts.** A hub is a cell of unique devices in the current window. One slow ping is enough. The cell is not a port.
- **Activity cells remain a grid.** A 0.05° cell is about 5 km. Same-island pairs are no longer shown as crossings. Cells on an unnamed coast stay inferred.
- **Playback window.** The window is `[t − W, t + 1)`. The bold trail is `(t − W, t]`. The selected vessel bypasses the speed filter and still counts toward hubs. Those are the original rules. Above 1× the clock commits one sample per frame, so GPS reports between samples are not drawn.
- **Basemap.** Geography comes from the CARTO vector style (OpenStreetMap and CARTO attribution). SeaPulse recolors water, land, roads, and labels. The style has no bathymetry, and house numbers start above the map’s maximum zoom. If the style fails to load, the data layers still draw on a flat sea-coloured background. Island labels use a font from that style, so they appear after the style loads.
